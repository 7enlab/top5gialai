Top 5 Gia Lai Specialties to Buy as Gifts, with Addresses and Public Prices

Coming home from the Gong Festival without a gift leaves half the story untold. This list only includes items that meet two conditions: a specific place to buy them in Pleiku, and a publicly listed reference price checked on 31 October 2026. The order runs from the most distinctive item to the easiest to buy; it is not a taste ranking, since taste depends on the person receiving the gift.

Read the prices correctly: they are reference prices from published guides, not shelf prices confirmed at the stall today. Festival-week stalls may charge more, and stalls inside the same market may differ. Use this list for the addresses, ask the price at the stall, and only then decide. Do not pay based on a price in this article alone.

How items were chosen

An item made the list only if it is tied to Gia Lai rather than common everywhere, can be carried home without cooking it on the spot, and has at least one buying address plus a public reference price in the sources checked. Dishes that are only eaten on site, with no address or price for a take-home version, were left out no matter how good they taste.

1. Bo mot nang Krong Pa (one-sun-dried beef) - the most unmistakably Gia Lai gift

Beef from the Krong Pa area, seasoned and dried for one sun, eaten with yellow ant salt, sour banana, star fruit and la e leaves. It suits a recipient who likes strong, savoury food, and it travels better than fresh produce.

  • Reference price: from 500,000 VND/kg (Tripi, Review Pleiku Gia Lai and Dulich9 all list this level).
  • Where to buy: Bo kho chi Ba, 154 Phu Dong, Pleiku.
  • When buying: ask whether it is one-sun dried or fully dried, since the two keep differently; buy it at the end of your trip, not the start, so it does not sit in luggage for days.

2. Yellow ant salt - distinctive, but only for a recipient you know can eat it

Yellow ant salt is made from yellow ants of the Krong Pa area, roasted with salt, lemongrass and la e leaves, then ground fine, and used as a dipping salt. It exists nowhere else in quite this form, but the flavour and the ingredient are unusual, so not everyone can eat it. Buy it only when you are sure the recipient enjoys trying strange foods; for a business contact or an older relative who has never tried it, the risk of it sitting unused is high.

  • Reference price: 250,000 VND/250 g (Tripi, Review Pleiku Gia Lai and Lu Hanh Viet Nam all list this level).
  • Where to buy: Pleiku Central Market, Dien Hong ward, Pleiku.
  • When buying: paired with the dried beef in item 1 it makes a proper set; a lone jar of salt with nothing to dip in it is hard to use.

3. Forest honey - the safest gift for older recipients

Gia Lai forest honey comes from bees working coffee flowers and forest flowers by season. Compared with the two items above, honey is the easiest gift: everyone can use it, no cooking knowledge is needed, and it keeps simply. The drawback is that forest honey is hard to tell from farmed or blended honey by looking at the jar, so buy from a reputable stall and ask about the source before paying.

  • Reference price: from 300,000 VND/bottle (Tripi, Review Pleiku Gia Lai, Dulich9 and Lu Hanh Viet Nam all list this level).
  • Where to buy: Pleiku Central Market, Dien Hong ward, Pleiku.
  • When buying: check the jar is sealed with no loose cap; honey is a liquid, so pack it against breakage and leakage before putting it in checked luggage.

4. Forest bamboo shoots - the cheapest gift, good for buying in quantity

Gia Lai forest bamboo shoots, mostly tre le bamboo, work in soups or stir-fries. This is the cheapest item on the list, suited to buying many portions for many people, or for a recipient who likes to cook. The drawback is that dried and fresh shoots keep differently, and fresh shoots are heavy and bulky next to the other items.

  • Reference price: from 80,000 VND/kg (Tripi, Review Pleiku Gia Lai and Tiphay all list this level).
  • Where to buy: Pleiku Central Market, Dien Hong ward, Pleiku.
  • When buying: ask whether the shoots are pre-boiled or raw, since keeping time differs; for a flight, prefer light dried shoots over fresh ones.

5. Ruou can (stem wine) - a strong cultural marker, but only practical by private car

Ruou can is the traditional stem wine of the Central Highlands peoples, served from a jar and drunk through stems. As a gift it carries the clearest cultural mark on this list, but it is also the bulkiest and most breakable. Buy it only with a private car or a solid packing plan; if flying, check liquid and checked-luggage rules before buying, not after.

  • Reference price: from 300,000 VND/jar (Tiphay; Lu Hanh Viet Nam lists around 300,000 VND/jar).
  • Where to buy: Ruou Can shop, 910 Le Duan, Chu A, Pleiku.
  • When buying: ask whether the price is for a small or large jar, since the listed price does not distinguish sizes; choose a small jar if the gift is symbolic.

Buying during festival week

Beyond the fixed addresses above, the OCOP and specialty market at Ia Gri village, Bien Ho commune, sells local products from 30 October to 1 November as part of the festival. That market suits browsing many items at once, but its prices still have to be asked at the stall; do not treat this article's prices as settled there. After 1 November, return to the fixed addresses in this list, since the festival market is over.

Quick picks

For one important person, where the Gia Lai mark matters most: dried beef with yellow ant salt. For an older recipient or someone unaccustomed to strange flavours: forest honey. For many recipients on a moderate budget: bamboo shoots in portions. For a cultural marker, travelling by private car: ruou can. If you do not know whether the recipient eats unusual foods, skip the yellow ant salt.
